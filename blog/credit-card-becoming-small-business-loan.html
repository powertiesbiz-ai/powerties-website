The Credit Card Is Becoming the Small Business Loan

By Keith Tully, Founder, PowerTies Consulting Group

The card statement lands on the desk. The owner pays it, well, part of it. The rest rides into next month, same as last month. Nothing about it feels like a decision. It is just how the month got covered.

A quiet habit is now the normal way to borrow

That quiet habit is becoming the way a lot of small businesses borrow. Not a bank loan, with paperwork and a payment book. Not a line of credit somebody planned out ahead of time. Just the card, reached for a little more each month, until the balance stops being a bridge and starts being the financing.

What the research found

Two surveys published this fall point in the same direction.

Gusto's 2026 State of Small Business report, released November 12, surveyed 1,731 owners of businesses with 24 or fewer employees. Among the owners who used outside financing this year, 79% relied on a business credit card. That is up from 69% the year before. And 63% of those card users do not pay the balance off each month. About a third of the owners surveyed, 35%, used no outside financing at all.

Intuit's QuickBooks Small Business Insights survey, in its October 2026 wave, tells the same story from the other side. It found that 68% of small businesses carry a credit card balance. The share that pay their balance in full every month has fallen from 58% in April 2024 to 32%. And 43% say they have become more reliant on cards in the past 12 months.

Read those numbers together and the picture is plain. The card is no longer just a handy way to pay for things. For a growing share of owners, the card is the loan.

Why that should get an owner's attention

A card is a handy tool for a purchase. It is an expensive way to run a business month after month. The interest on a carried balance is some of the most expensive money a business can borrow, and it quietly raises the cost of everything that balance paid for.

There is a second problem, and it is the one owners feel without naming it. When the card covers the month, it can also cover up a question. Some of the work that balance paid for earned its keep. Some of it did not. As long as the balance never gets paid down, nobody has to sort out which is which.

The calmer question

Before January's statement arrives, there is a calmer question to answer: which of your services actually make money, hour by hour, and which ones are quietly being carried by the card?

That is a knowable thing. It is not a feeling, and it does not take a finance degree. It is your prices, your hours, and your costs, service by service, laid out plainly.
