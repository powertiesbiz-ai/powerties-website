A Small Business Owner's Plain Guide to ChatGPT

By Keith Tully, Founder, PowerTies Consulting Group

You signed up for ChatGPT, opened the blank box, typed a question or two, got a polite answer that could belong to any business, and closed the tab. The account is still there. The useful part never started.

That is the most common story I hear. The tool is not hard to open. It is hard to know what to ask it, in words that give you something you can actually use.

As the founder of PowerTies Consulting Group, here is the plain way to start. I tell owners to treat ChatGPT like a fast drafting helper who knows nothing about your business until you tell it. Give it your facts and the form you need, and the answers get useful quickly.

How a good prompt is built

A good prompt has four parts. Say what you need, who it is for, the real details from your business, and what form the answer should take, such as a short email or a checklist.

For example, instead of just asking for a follow-up email, try this. Write a short follow-up to a homeowner who got an estimate three days ago and has not replied. Keep it polite and brief. Offer two times for a quick call. Use only the details I give you below.

Then paste your real details under it. The better the facts, the less generic the draft. You still edit and send it yourself.

Ten prompts for real owner work

Here are ten starting prompts you can copy and adjust. Each one solves a small, repeating task.

1. The follow-up email. Draft a brief follow-up after an estimate, in a friendly tone, with one clear next step and no pressure.

2. The rough notes cleanup. Turn these job notes into a clean summary a customer could read, with the work done, what comes next, and anything they need to decide.

3. The review reply. Draft a calm reply to this customer review. Thank them for the specific thing they mentioned, and keep it short.

4. The difficult message. Help me rewrite this message so it is firm and polite. I need to say the start date has to move, and I want to keep the relationship strong.

5. The service description. Write a plain description of this service for our website, in words a first time customer would understand, with no hype.

6. The meeting summary. Summarize this call in five bullet points, then list the decisions and who owns each next step.

7. The estimate draft starter. Turn these measurements and notes into a first draft estimate I can price and correct. Leave the prices blank for me to fill in.

8. The FAQ answer. A customer keeps asking this question. Draft a clear answer I can reuse, in our voice, in under one hundred words.

9. The job post helper. Draft a help wanted post for this role, with the real duties, the hours, and the kind of person who does well here.

10. The week planner. Here is my list of unfinished tasks. Group them into what must happen this week, what can wait, and what I should hand off, and explain your thinking briefly.

Notice what these have in common. None of them ask ChatGPT to run your business. Each one asks for a first draft on one clear task, which you then check, correct, and use. That is the right size for this tool.

What never to paste into ChatGPT

Never paste customer payment details, passwords, or private financial records. Never paste full customer lists or private personnel matters.

A simple test helps. If you would not read it aloud to a stranger in your shop, do not paste it in. Use general descriptions where you can, and keep sensitive facts in your own files. The draft will still be useful.

Also check every fact ChatGPT gives you back. It can write smoothly and still get a date, a rule, or a product detail wrong. Treat anything it states about laws, taxes, or prices as a lead to verify, not a final answer.

Make it sound like you

Give ChatGPT a short sample of how you already write, such as a past email you liked, and ask it to match that tone. Tell it what to avoid. No hype, no big claims, and no words you would never say to a customer in person.

Then keep a small file of prompts that worked. In a month you will have your own short playbook, built from real work, instead of starting from a blank box each time.
