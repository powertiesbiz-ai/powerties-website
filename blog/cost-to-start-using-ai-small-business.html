How Much Does It Cost to Start Using AI in a Small Business?

By Keith Tully, Founder, PowerTies Consulting Group

"How much does AI cost?" sounds like a simple question. It has three answers, because there are three different costs, and only one of them shows up on a price page.

As the founder of PowerTies Consulting Group, owners ask me this before almost every working session, so here is the breakdown I give them.

Cost one: the tools

This is the part owners expect to be expensive, and it is usually the cheapest part.

Most of the AI assistants worth paying for charge about $20 per person per month. That gets you the current generation of tools that write, summarize, and answer questions well. Specialized tools cost more. Software that answers your phones, manages your schedule, or runs your follow-ups might run $30 to a few hundred a month depending on what it replaces.

For most small businesses, the first tool or two together land somewhere between $20 and $100 a month. Real money, but smaller than most owners feared, and smaller than one hour of the owner's own time is worth.

Cost two: your time

This is the cost nobody puts on the invoice, and it is the one that decides whether AI pays off.

Every tool takes setup. Someone has to teach it your prices, your services, and your way of saying things. Then there is a learning stretch, usually a few weeks, where the tool feels slower than doing it yourself. Owners who expect magic in week one quit in week two.

Here is the plain truth from watching businesses do this: the most expensive AI tool is the one you pay for and never open. A $20 tool used every day is cheap. A $200 tool abandoned by March is the waste.

Budget your time honestly. Two or three focused hours to set up the first tool, then fifteen minutes at a time as you fold it into the week.

Cost three: help, if you buy it

You can do all of this yourself, and many owners do. If you buy help instead, here is the landscape.

Most AI consultants charge $150 to $500 an hour, or $2,000 to $10,000 and up for a project engagement. At those rates, an owner should ask hard questions about what exactly gets delivered.

The order matters more than the amount

A $20 tool aimed at your biggest time drain will beat $300 a month of tools aimed at nothing in particular. That is why the report I mentioned ranks opportunities by impact per dollar. Start where the payback is fastest, let the savings from the first win pay for the second step, and never buy the whole shelf at once.

A sane first budget

If you want a number to write down, use this one.

  • One tool: about $20 to $50 a month.
  • Your setup time: two to three hours, once.
  • One repeating task as the test case.
  • Thirty days before you judge it.

Total risk: less than the cost of one lost job from a slow estimate or an unanswered phone. Judge the test by hours handed back, not by how impressive the tool feels.

If the hours come back, expand. If they do not, you have learned something cheap, and you stop. Either way, you will know what AI costs your business. Not from a price page. From experience.
