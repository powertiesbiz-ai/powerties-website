What Can AI Actually Do for a Small Business?

By Keith Tully, Founder, PowerTies Consulting Group

Almost every week, a business owner asks me some version of the same question. They have heard the hype. They have seen the demos. And they still cannot tell what any of it means for a company with six employees and a phone that never stops ringing.

As the founder of PowerTies Consulting Group, I spend my working days helping owners sort the useful from the noise. Here is the honest list I give them.

First, what AI is not

AI is not a robot employee. It will not run your business while you watch. It is closer to a very fast junior assistant. It drafts, summarizes, organizes, and reminds. It works from what you give it, and a person still checks its work before anything reaches a customer.

Once you see it that way, the useful list gets clear fast.

The everyday wins

These are the tasks that eat an owner's week in small pieces.

  • First drafts of almost everything. Emails, quotes, proposals, job posts, and letters. You describe what you need, AI writes the rough version, and you fix it instead of starting from a blank page.
  • Summaries. A long email thread, a sales call, a stack of notes from a job site. AI can turn them into a short summary with the decisions and next steps pulled out.
  • Turning rough notes into clean documents. The scribbled list from a walkthrough becomes a checklist, a scope of work, or a follow-up letter your customer can actually read.

None of this is glamorous. That is the point. An hour saved on paperwork is an hour that can go to customers.

The customer-facing wins

  • Answering the same ten questions. Every business gets asked about hours, prices, service areas, and timelines, over and over. AI can draft those answers in your words, ready for you to approve and send.
  • Following up after an estimate. More quotes die from silence than from price. AI can write the follow-up messages and remind you when to send them, so a good quote does not quietly expire.
  • Review replies and posts. AI can draft a response to a Google review or a caption for a photo of finished work. You keep the final say. The draft just removes the blank-page problem.

The big chains already use AI for work like this. They schedule staff with it, predict what inventory they will need, and answer routine customer questions with it. A few years ago that took custom software and a big budget. Today the same kind of tool rents by the month, priced for a small shop.

The money wins

  • Faster estimates. The business that answers first often wins the job. If a quote that took an evening now takes twenty minutes, you bid more work with the same day you already have.
  • Invoices that chase themselves. AI can draft the reminder messages and keep the list of who owes what, so getting paid stops depending on your memory.
  • Finding leaks. Feed AI your list of services, prices, and past jobs, and it can help you spot the work you give away free, the follow-ups nobody made, and the offers you stopped mentioning. Small leaks, fixed monthly, add up.

What AI cannot do for you

It cannot make the judgment call. It cannot read a difficult customer the way you can after twenty years. It cannot build trust, and trust is the whole business at this size.

It also cannot know things you never told it. AI works from your prices, your process, and your words. Setting that up is real work, done once, by a person.

Where to start

Start with the task you hate that repeats every week. Not the biggest problem in the business. The repeating one. Give that one task to one tool for thirty days and judge it by a simple measure: how many hours did it hand back?
